# Review: graphics-3d

## Verdict
The page is ready once the fixes below are made. The model is a real OpenGL-style pipeline: lookAt and perspective matrices, perspective divide, edge-function coverage, z-buffer, and perspective-correct barycentric interpolation of u/w, v/w, 1/w. The ray tracer is a real Whitted-style one: Möller–Trumbore intersection, shadow rays, reflection, Snell refraction with total internal reflection, and Schlick's Fresnel term. Both share one camera, one set of triangles and one shading function, and the tests check real properties (Snell, the 41.8° critical angle, 4% Fresnel reflection, the 1/d perspective scaling, and the rasteriser and ray tracer seeing the same object in every pixel). The most important fix is on the perspective step: it says a narrow field of view "flattens" the scene. The camera doesn't move when the slider changes, so a narrow field of view only crops and enlarges the middle of the picture. The flattened look of a zoom lens comes from where the camera stands, so the text repeats a well-known misconception. The other fixes are small: a readout label that leaves out a factor, a partly wrong PlayStation explanation, and two loose descriptions of what the bounce and z-buffer controls show.

## Factual errors
- **High**, steps.ts:193. "A narrow one (a zoom lens) makes the scene fill the picture and flattens it". The `fov` slider changes only the projection; the eye stays where it is. So a narrow view is a crop of the wide one, scaled up, and near and far objects keep the same relative sizes. The squeezed-together look of telephoto shots comes from standing far away, not from the focal length. The second half of the sentence ("a wide one … stretches what is near the edges") is correct. Fix: "Change the field of view. A narrow one (a zoom lens) enlarges the middle of the same view: the camera hasn't moved, so near and far things keep their proportions and the picture is just a crop of the wide one. (The flattened look of telephoto photos comes from standing far away.) A wide one shows more and stretches what is near the edges." Sources: https://petapixel.com/2021/08/02/lenses-dont-cause-perspective-distortion-and-lens-compression/, https://photographylife.com/what-is-distortion
- **Medium**, steps.ts:250. "The first PlayStation (1994) drew textures without this correction, which is why its floors and walls wobbled as the camera moved." Missing perspective correction made PS1 textures warp and swim. The wobble of the polygons themselves is a separate artefact: vertices were snapped to whole pixels, with no sub-pixel precision. Fix: "…drew textures without this correction, which is why its textures warped and swam across floors and walls as the camera moved (the jittering of the polygons themselves is a separate effect: their corners snapped to whole pixels)." Sources: https://pikuma.com/blog/how-to-make-ps1-graphics, https://www.howtogeek.com/why-did-the-playstation-1-have-wobbly-graphics/
- **Low**, steps.ts:208. "The three edge-function values, divided by the triangle's area, are the pixel's barycentric coordinates". Each edge function is *twice* the signed area of a sub-triangle, so the divisor is twice the triangle's area. The model is right: it divides by `area = edge(...)`, which is 2× the area. Fix: "…divided by the same quantity for the whole triangle (twice its area), are…"
- **Low**, steps.ts:222. "Real graphics cards also skip triangles that face away…". Back-face culling is a pipeline option that the application turns on; the card doesn't always do it. Fix: "Real renderers usually also tell the graphics card to skip triangles…"

Everything else checks out: Phong 1975 (CACM 18(6), https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/360825.360839), Whitted 1980 (CACM 23(6), https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/358876.358882), Lambert's cos 60° = ½, Schlick's ≈4% at normal incidence for n = 1.5, the 41.8° critical angle, the view-matrix rows (right, up, backward), w = depth, NDC z from −1 to 1, and the 654-triangle count (icosphere level 2 = 320).

## Model issues
- **Low**, render.ts:629–663 and 714–717. `coverage` and `rasterise` count a pixel as inside when an edge function is ≥ 0 on all three edges, with no tie-break. A pixel centre that lies exactly on an edge shared by two triangles is therefore counted for both. Real GPUs use a fill rule (D3D/OpenGL "top-left") so that such a pixel belongs to exactly one triangle. With the z-buffer on, the picture is unaffected (the second fragment loses the `z >= depth` test), but `fragments`/`hidden` slightly over-count, and the docstring's "the edge-function test a GPU makes" isn't quite accurate. Fix: change the docstring to "the edge-function test (without the tie-breaking fill rule a GPU adds for pixels exactly on a shared edge)", or add a top-left bias. Sources: https://fgiesen.wordpress.com/2013/02/08/triangle-rasterization-in-practice/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasterisation

Otherwise the model is sound. The near-plane drop really can't happen: the distance slider's minimum of 6.5 is larger than the 5.53 from the target to the farthest floor corner. Normals are transformed with the model matrix, which is correct because the scaling is uniform. Interpolating depth linearly in screen space is correct for z/w. The linearisation in `depthImage` matches the inverse of the OpenGL depth mapping. Inside the glass, Schlick uses the transmitted angle, as it should.

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **Medium**, RasterScene.svelte:337. The label reads "Phong highlight (r · eye)^shine: + X". The value is `highlight()`, which is `ks · (r·v)^shininess`, with ks = 0.8 for the mirror, 0.3 for the cube and 0.08 for the floor. A reader who checks the formula gets a different number. Fix: `text="Phong highlight k·(r · v)^shine: + {fmt(hit.highlight)}"`, and add a muted line `k = {ks}, shine = {shin}` (export `SPECULAR` from render.ts).
- **Low**, RasterScene.svelte:325. "Lambert: n · l = cos {θ}° = {max(0, n·l)}" shows things like "cos 100° = 0.00" when the lamp is behind the surface. Fix: `text="Lambert: max(0, cos {Math.round(hit.angle)}°) = {fmt(Math.max(0, hit.nl))}"`.
- **Medium**, steps.ts:303. The bounce paragraph never says what level 2 shows (the glass ball seen directly becomes clear). It then calls the level-3 effect "reflections of reflections", but the test (e2e:238–257) checks that light that went *through* the glass becomes visible in the mirror. That is a refraction seen in a reflection. Fix: "…At 1 the mirror shows the sky and the floor, but the glass is still dark inside: light needs two refractions to get through it, in and out. At 2 the glass clears and shows a small, upside-down picture of what lies behind it. At 3, the glass ball seen in the mirror clears too: light bent twice, then reflected." (The upside-down image is what a ball lens with n = 1.5 does: its focus is about 0.83 units from the centre, so the scene behind it is well beyond focus.)
- **Low**, steps.ts:303. "At 0 the mirror is dark and the glass is black". At 0 bounces the glass still shows its own Phong highlight (render.ts:1032). Fix: "…and the glass is black apart from its highlight."
- **Low**, steps.ts:221. "The cube's far faces and the balls' back halves come out on top." For the cube this holds from the default view, because the faces are drawn in the order +x, −x, +y, −y, +z, −z. Each ball's triangles, though, come in subdivision order, grouped by the 20 original faces, so back and front triangles alternate. The result should be a patchwork, not whole back halves; I'm moderately confident of this from reading the code, without having rendered it. Fix: "…The cube's far faces and patches of the balls' back halves come out on top."

The quoted numbers match the model: 654 triangles, about 50,000 pixel tests and about 2.5 million ray–triangle tests (both asserted in e2e:186–191), 23,040 rays, and a ratio of about 50.

## Catalogue requirements
The catalogue requirements are met. The camera and lamp can be moved, by dragging on the map or with sliders. One view projects and fills triangles (with a step that builds the picture triangle by triangle), and the other traces a ray per pixel with shadow rays and an adjustable bounce limit. The Compare scene shows the takeaway in the graphic itself: two pictures side by side, ✓/✗ for shadows, reflections and light through glass, and the test counts drawn to one scale. Prerequisites (linear-transformations, light-optics) are linked in the text. There is no Pitfall or Needs field, and the scene is generated rather than invented data.

## Clarity
- **Low**, steps.ts:250. "Texel" ("just takes the nearest texel") is never defined. Fix: "this page just takes the nearest texel (texture pixel)."
- **Low**, WorldScene.svelte:262. The result of the projection is captioned "clip space", a term the perspective step never uses or explains. Fix: change the caption to `"after projection"`, or add one clause to steps.ts:192: "…copies the depth into the fourth number, w (the result is called clip space)."
