# Review: consensus-blockchains

## Verdict
The page is ready once the fixes below are made. The models are faithful: OM(m) follows Lamport–Shostak–Pease, Raft has the correct vote, log-matching and current-term commit rules, the retarget copies `pow.cpp` including the 2015-gap off-by-one, the hashing is real double SHA-256, and Nakamoto's §11 code is transcribed and checked against his tables. Most of the claims are correct. The most important fix is in the double-spend note. It says Nakamoto's Poisson shortcut "slightly" underestimates the risk, but at the page's own settings it is out by a factor of 5. The "below 0.1 % after N blocks" readout uses that low figure, while the exact value drawn right next to it says otherwise.

## Factual errors
- **High**, steps.ts:304 (and RaceView.svelte:98, 305, 327). "With 30 % it takes 24 … which slightly underestimates the risk" is misleading. I computed both formulas from the model's own code. At q = 0.3, z = 24, Nakamoto gives 0.081 % and the exact race gives 0.40 % (5×). At q = 0.1, z = 10 the gap is 6.3×. With the exact formula the 0.1 % threshold is 6 blocks at 10 % and 32 blocks at 30 %, not 5 and 24. At z = 1 Nakamoto is actually slightly *above* the exact value (20.46 % vs 20 %). Replace the last two sentences of the note with: "For an attacker with 10 % of the power, Nakamoto's formula needs 5 blocks to bring the risk below 0.1 %, and 24 with 30 %. … Nakamoto approximates the attacker's progress with a Poisson distribution. That underestimates the risk, by more the longer the shop waits: with 30 % of the power, 24 blocks still leave a 0.4 % chance, and the exact value (Rosenfeld, 2014; dashed) needs 32 blocks to get below 0.1 %. The simulated races follow the exact value." In RaceView, compute `needed` from the exact formula, e.g. `confirmationsFor(q, risk = 0.001, max = 1000, f = exactRace)` with `if (f(q, z) < risk)`. The label would then read "Risk below 0.1 % after {needed} blocks (exact)". Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2009 (Rosenfeld, eq. 1, which the model's `exactRace` implements).
- **Medium**, steps.ts:250 ("The header has one free field, the nonce") and the dfn of nonce. In Bitcoin the nonce is only 32 bits, so a miner runs through all of it long before finding a block, then changes other fields. The Bitcoin wiki says the nonce is small at 32-bits so each time it wraps the extraNonce field must be incremented (or otherwise changed) to avoid repeating work. Replace with: "The header has a field kept free for this, the nonce (real miners run through its 4 billion values quickly and then also change a spare field in the first transaction)". Source: https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Block_hashing_algorithm
- **Low/medium**, steps.ts:316, "those who sign two conflicting blocks lose them". Ethereum destroys only part of the stake. ethereum.org says signing two conflicting blocks results in slashing: part of the validator's stake is destroyed and the validator is forcibly removed. Replace with "lose part of them and are expelled". Source: https://ethereum.org/staking/
- **Low**, steps.ts:182, "With m traitors it runs m + 1 rounds of relaying". OM(m) has m + 1 rounds of messages: the order, then m relay rounds. The scene itself shows one relay round for m = 1. Replace with "it runs m + 1 rounds of messages: the order, then m rounds of relaying".
- **Low**, steps.ts:239, the list of header fields leaves out the version. Add "a version number,". Source: https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Block_hashing_algorithm
- **Low**, steps.ts:290 (dfn "orphaned") and ForkView labels. Strictly, a block that loses the race is a *stale* block; an "orphan" is a block whose parent is unknown. Popular usage mixes the two. Lightspark's glossary: An orphan block is a valid block whose parent is temporarily unknown, while a stale block is a valid block that was solved correctly but ultimately not included in the longest chain. Add to the dfn: "(often called orphaned; strictly, a stale block)". Source: https://www.lightspark.com/glossary/stale-block

Everything else checks out: the 1982 TOPLAS paper, FLP 1985, Raft 2014 and its election timeouts are chosen randomly from a fixed interval (e.g., 150–300ms), PBFT 1999, Paxos 1998, etcd, the 2009 launch, 2016 blocks in 14 days, the ×4 clamp, the 51 % claim, the six-block convention and the September 2022 Merge. Decker & Wattenhofer is quoted correctly: The median time until a node receives a block is 6.5 seconds whereas the mean is at 12.6 seconds. The same paper gives the fork rate r= 1.69%.

## Model issues
- **Medium**, steps.ts:291 and e2e:249–258. The note says "For small delays d, about d ÷ 600 s of blocks end up orphaned", but the model gives about 0.75 × d/600. I ported `forkRun` and ran 60 seeds × 400 blocks: 1.25 % at 10 s (vs 1.67 %), 6.9 % at 60 s (vs 10 %), 13 % at 120 s (vs 20 %). The model is right and the note is wrong. A pool never forks against its own block, so the rate is about (1 − Σ share²) · d/600, which is 0.775 · d/600 for these shares. Replace the sentence with: "For small delays d, a little under d ÷ 600 s of blocks end up orphaned (about 0.8 × d ÷ 600 s here, because a pool never races against its own block)." The test's ±40 % band around d/600 hides this gap. Tighten it to ±20 % around `(1 - Σ s²) * d / 600`.
- **Low**, consensus.ts:664–674. Bitcoin compares the hash read as a little-endian number, so the zeros it requires are at the *end* of the raw digest. Real block hashes are shown byte-reversed so that the zeros appear first. Counting leading zeros of the raw digest is statistically identical, but the "difficulty in zero bits" simplification should mention it in a code comment.
- **Low**, consensus.ts:24 says the simplification "Raft clients always find the leader they can reach" is stated on the page, but steps.ts never mentions it. Add to the election notes: "Clients here always know which server is the leader; real clients are redirected or retry."

Otherwise the Raft, OM, retarget and race models behave correctly at the edges (z = 0, q = 0.5, all followers down, a cut that never heals).

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **Medium**, GeneralsScene.svelte:20–28 and steps.ts:181. With "Who betrays: the commander", every case below 3f + 1 shows a green ✓ (I ran the model on all of them: n = 3 f = 1, and n = 3–6 with f = 2). For n = 3, OM(1) with a "tie means retreat" rule can never be beaten by a lying commander, because both loyal lieutenants see the same two values. The impossibility shows only when a lieutenant lies. The reader sees "it works" next to a red "needs at least 4 generals". When `f > 0 && n < need && run.ok`, append to the rule line: " · this lie happened to fail, but another could succeed: nothing guarantees it". Change the select's help to: "A lying commander alone cannot split three generals; the failure shows when a lieutenant lies."
- **Low**, RetargetView.svelte:55–61 and steps.ts:97, 276, "from week 4". Period 3 (0-based index 2) starts after 4 weeks, which is week 5. Use "after 4 weeks" (label: "Hash rate after week 4") or "from week 5".
- **Low**, GeneralsScene.svelte:81. With a single loyal lieutenant (n = 3, f = 1, a lieutenant traitor), the verdict "They agree, but not with their loyal commander" is vacuous. When `run.loyal.length === 1`, show "L1 disobeys his loyal commander".
- **Low**, steps.ts:196. The scene restarts the crashed leader at 18 s and shows it on the timeline, but the text never says so. Add: "It restarts at 18 s, sees the higher term and rejoins as a follower."

## Catalogue requirements
The entry is fully met. You can crash the leader or followers and cut the network, and the cluster visibly keeps agreeing or stalls. Blocks are chained by hash, and tampering with block 1 invalidates the later blocks. Mining searches nonces at an adjustable difficulty. The takeaway (voting among known machines versus proof-of-work among strangers) is drawn on its own in the summary scene. The page links its prerequisites, internet and cryptography. There is no Needs or Pitfall field.
