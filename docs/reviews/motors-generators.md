# Review: motors-generators

## Verdict
The page is ready once the fixes below are made. I ported the model to Python and re-ran it, and its physics checks out. The force, torque and couple formulas are right, the emf equals −dΦ/dt, and the commutator's sign logic agrees with the brush and half-ring geometry in both scenes. The numbers quoted in the text are reproduced: 3.75 N, 0.375 N·m, 3.14 V, 0.84 turns/s and 0.32 A (I got 0.842 turns/s and 0.323 A), and doubling the voltage gives 2.04× the speed. **The most important fix is the page's first sentence.** It says almost every motor and power-station generator is a coil turning between magnet poles. That is false for power-station generators, where the magnet turns inside fixed coils (the page's own note on the `ac` step says so), and for induction motors, the most common motors in industry.

## Factual errors
- **High**, steps.ts:133. The claim "Inside almost every electric motor, and almost every power-station generator, is the same idea: a coil of wire that can turn on a shaft between the poles of a magnet" is wrong in two ways. Large generators have a rotating field and a stationary armature, and steps.ts:216 says exactly that. Induction motors, the most widely used industrial motors, have no turning coil between magnet poles. **Fix:** "Inside almost every electric motor and generator is the same idea: coils of wire and a magnetic field moving relative to each other. The simplest version, the one on this page, is a coil of wire that turns on a shaft between the poles of a magnet." Sources: https://instrumentationtools.com/types-of-ac-generators/ (large generators use a rotating field and stationary armature); https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/ac-induction-motor (induction motors are the most widely used in industry).
- **Medium**, steps.ts:148 (body and the "dead points" definition). The text says that at the dead points "the forces pull straight outwards" and "point straight away from the shaft". With the default current that is only true at 180°. In the model at θ = 0°, side a is at the top and its force points down, towards the shaft (`sideForce(−0.75, true).y = −3.75`). So at 0°/360° the forces squeeze the coil inwards, and the reader can see that on the graphic. **Fix:** body: "there the forces point straight along the coil, towards or away from the shaft, and cannot turn it"; definition: "…the forces on its sides point along the coil, through the shaft, and cannot turn it."
- **Medium**, steps.ts:228. "The first power stations, in the 1880s, used dynamos" is not accurate. The world's first public supply, at Godalming in 1881, was an AC system using a Siemens alternator. **Fix:** "many of the first power stations, such as Edison's Pearl Street Station in New York (1882), used dynamos". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mains_electricity, https://ethw.org/Milestones:Pearl_Street_Station,_1882
- **Low**, steps.ts:243. "The work you do turning the generator equals the electrical energy the lamp receives" is not quite right. In the model, `LAMP_R = 10 Ω` is the coil plus the lamp, so some of the energy heats the coil's own wire even without friction. **Fix:** "…equals the electrical energy delivered to the circuit (the lamp, plus a little that heats the coil's own wire), turn for turn."

## Model issues
- **Medium**, MachineScene.svelte:168. When turning by hand, `handOmega` returns the old `last.w` whenever dt ≤ 0, and it does not update `last.a`. If the timeline is paused, or `t` stops when the step's `duration` runs out (I can't tell from the framework code), dragging the crank changes θ but not ω. The meter then shows no voltage, or a stale one, while the reader is turning the coil, which breaks "while the coil turns, a voltage appears". I am about 60% confident this is reachable. **Fix:** use a wall clock for the hand-crank speed (`performance.now()` inside `moveCrank`/`keyCrank`), independent of `t`.
- **Low**, steps.ts:162. The note credits the dying away of the rocking only to bearing and brush friction. In the model, the slip-ring coil's own induced current also brakes it, with a coefficient of (NBA)²sin²θ/R ≤ 0.0625 N·m·s. That is several times the 0.012 bearing friction over most of the swing. **Fix:** "The rocking dies away because of friction, and because the moving coil's own induced voltage (see the next steps) drives currents that turn some of the motion into heat."
- **Low**, steps.ts:136 / 189. The code assumes zero coil inductance and instant commutation (machine.ts:38–39), but the page doesn't say so, and the catalogue asks for simplifications to be stated on the page. **Fix:** append to `MODEL`: "The coil's inductance is ignored, so the current switches instantly at the commutator (in real motors this causes sparking at the brushes)."
- **Low**, e2e test:59–70. The stuck test checks only where the coil ends up. It doesn't check the overshoot and rocking the narrative promises. My port swings to 224° and crosses 180° six times. **Fix:** add `expect(max θ over the run).toBeGreaterThan(Math.PI + 10*deg)`. Also add a steady-state power check: mean V·I = mean I²R + mean friction power.
- **Low**, MachineScene.svelte:22 vs :70; Contacts.svelte:50 vs :58. These comments are stale. The header says the lamp generator turns at 1 turn/s, but `GEN_SPEED = 2`. The slip-ring comment says "near face while cos > 0", but the code tests `< 0`. Fix both comments.

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **Low**, steps.ts:161. "The battery keeps pushing 0.75 A through it" is only true at the default 3 V, and this step has a voltage slider. **Fix:** "…while the battery keeps pushing current through it (0.75 A at 3 V) and turns it all into heat."
- Everything else checked matches. The ⊙/⊗ marks, the force arrows, "left side always into the page", the commutator trace never going below zero, the 0.75 A "held still" line, the polarity of the voltmeter's + terminal, and the Lenz torque in `same` all agree with what the model computes.

## Catalogue requirements
The interaction is built as the entry describes: the coil turned by forces, the commutator reversing the current every half-turn, hand-cranking with AC through slip rings and DC through the commutator. The takeaway is shown in the graphic on the `same` step, and there is no Pitfall or Needs field to check.

## Clarity
- **Low**, steps.ts:215. "Alternates 50 times a second" is ambiguous, because at 50 Hz the current reverses 100 times a second. **Fix:** "Mains electricity goes through 50 full cycles a second in Europe and most of the world, and 60 in North America."
- **Low**, steps.ts:214. The body uses ω and gets 3.14 V without saying what ω is; that only appears in the note. **Fix:** "…and how fast it turns, ω (2π for one turn a second)."
