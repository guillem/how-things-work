# Review: semiconductors

## Verdict
The page is ready after the fixes below. The physics is sound and the numbers hold up. I recomputed ρᵢ ≈ 3.4 × 10⁵ Ω·cm (2 × 10¹¹ × copper), 2.4 million times more conductive at 1 ppm P, V_bi = 0.835 V, W = 0.147 µm, I(0.7 V) = 575 µA, 59.5 mV per decade and E_c − E_F = 0.14 eV on the n side. The Masetti parameters and the depletion-approximation formulas are implemented correctly. Fix these first:
1. The levels step tells the reader "the band keeps the same width" while the graphic visibly widens it.
2. In the doping scene, the compensation transfer the acceptor step promises never animates, because every animation is tied to step time rather than to when an atom was placed.
3. The reverse-current explanation describes the minority carriers moving the wrong way.

## Factual errors
- **medium**, steps.ts:233: "minority carriers (holes that wander into the n side, electrons in the p side)". Holes crossing *into* the n side are forward injection. The reverse current comes from minority carriers made *in* each side that drift *to* the junction. Replace with: "…from the very rare minority carriers: holes made by heat in the n side and electrons made in the p side, which wander to the edge of the depletion zone and are swept across by the field."
- **medium**, steps.ts:235 (and the claims at 233 and 265): "Real diodes also leak a little more in reverse…" This understates it badly. In silicon at room temperature, generation in the depletion zone dominates the reverse current, by orders of magnitude over the ideal 10⁻¹⁵ A. A 1N4148 is specified at 25 nA at −20 V. Replace with: "Real silicon diodes leak far more in reverse than the ideal equation says (nanoamps for a common 1N4148), mostly from pairs generated by heat inside the depletion zone; the 10⁻¹⁵ A drawn here is the ideal diode's." Sources: https://www.eng.auburn.edu/~wilambm/pap/2011/K10147_C008.pdf, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1N4148_signal_diode
- **medium**, steps.ts:267: "Add a third doped region whose voltage controls the junctions and you have a transistor, billions of which make every computer chip." A third doped region makes a bipolar transistor. Chips are built from MOSFETs, where an insulated gate electrode controls a channel. Replace with: "Add a third doped region and you have a bipolar transistor; put an insulated electrode over the region between two junctions and its voltage opens a conducting channel: the MOSFET, billions of which make every computer chip." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOSFET
- **medium**, steps.ts:123: holes are slower "because each step needs a bound electron to hop". The bond picture is fine as a metaphor, but the physical reason is the valence band's larger effective mass (heavy holes ≈ 0.46 m₀) and stronger scattering. Replace with: "…because the valence-band states holes occupy respond more sluggishly to a field (a larger effective mass)." I am moderately confident this matters at Level 2. Source: http://apachepersonal.miun.se/~gorthu/halvledare/Effective%20mass%20in%20semiconductors.htm
- **low**, steps.ts:162: "fewer than one stray atom in a billion". Czochralski wafers hold about 10¹⁷–10¹⁸ cm⁻³ of interstitial oxygen (around 10 ppm), but it is electrically inactive. Write "fewer than one *electrically active* stray atom in a billion". Source: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0953-8984/12/25/201
- **low**, steps.ts:162: "about three times more resistive". The model, which matches measured data, gives a ratio of 3.0 at 10¹⁵, 2.3 at 10¹⁷ and 1.6 at 10¹⁹ cm⁻³. Write "two to three times more resistive at light and moderate doping".

## Model issues
- **low**, model.ts:134 and steps.ts:162: full ionisation is said to hold "up to about 10¹⁸ cm⁻³", yet the chart runs to 3 × 10¹⁹ on the same assumption. The result is still roughly right, because above the Mott transition (~4 × 10¹⁸ for P) the donor electrons conduct anyway. Add to the note: "above ~10¹⁸ the donor levels merge with the band, so their electrons still conduct." I am moderately confident.
- **low**, e2e test: nothing checks the Fermi-level placement the junction scene draws. Add `expect(GAPS.Si.gap/2 - KT*Math.log(JUNCTION_DOPING/NI_SI)).toBeGreaterThan(0.1)`, so that E_F stays below E_c at 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ (non-degenerate).
- Otherwise the constants, units (F/cm → cm → µm), Masetti formula and coefficients, mass-action solver, parabolic band profile and tight-binding levels all check out.

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **medium**, steps.ts:53, CrystalScene.svelte:315–318 ("The band keeps the same width" / "Its width does not grow"): in the chain model the spread is 4β·cos(π/(N+1)). That is 2β at N = 2, 3.2β at N = 4 and 3.8β at N = 8, so the drawn band visibly widens as the slider moves.
  - Replace the body sentence with: "the number of levels grows, but the band widens only at first and never exceeds a fixed width set by how strongly neighbours overlap."
  - Replace the scene text with `${N} atoms: each level splits into ${N}. The band widens towards a fixed limit.`
  - Replace the N ≥ 16 text with "…a continuous band. Its width has stopped growing."
- **medium**, DopeScene.svelte:105–170 against steps.ts:122 ("the phosphorus's spare electron drops straight into the boron's hole"): `transfers` uses `smoothstep(0.4, 1.6, time)` and donors free at `time ≥ 0.9`, both counted from the start of the step. Any atom the reader adds later is ionised or compensated instantly, so the promised transfer and the "circles it loosely, then leaves" behaviour never show, and new free electrons appear mid-wander away from their donor. Fix: keep `const placed = new Map<number, number>()`, call `placed.set(i, t)` in `toggle()`, and use a local clock per site: `const age = (i) => time - (placed.get(i) ?? 0)`. Use `age(d)` in place of `time` for the electrons and `ionised`. For transfers use `u = smoothstep(0.4, 1.6, Math.min(age(donors[k]), age(acceptors[k])))`, and use `age(acc)` for holes.
- **low**, steps.ts:210: "the electrons on the n side sit higher in energy than the holes' level on the p side" mixes electrons with a level. Write: "the electrons' quasi-Fermi level (n side) sits higher than the holes' (p side) by exactly qV."
- Everything else matches what the scenes compute and show: 0.84 eV, 0.15 µm, half a milliampere at 0.7 V, more than 10¹¹ between reverse and forward, 5 trillion, 2,000×, ×10 per 60 mV.
