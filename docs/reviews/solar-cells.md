# Review: solar-cells

## Verdict
The page is ready after the fixes below. I re-ran the model independently with the same constants and the same detailed-balance formula. For the ideal silicon cell I get V_oc 0.877 V, V_mp 0.787 V, J_mp 42.4 mA/cm², fill factor 0.870 and efficiency 33.4%. The GaAs voltage ratio comes out at 0.975, the temperature coefficients at −0.90 mV/°C and −0.15%/°C, and silicon at 1000 suns at 41%. These match every number the page quotes. The physics is right, and the catalogue's pitfall is handled well: the voltage loss is computed from the cell's glow, and the dashed ceiling is shown next to the limit. The most important fix is in the `mpp` step. It describes V_oc × J_sc as "the largest [rectangle] you could draw under the curve", which is false. The largest rectangle under the curve is the maximum-power rectangle itself, so on that reading the fill factor would always be 100%. The second fix is the Shockley–Queisser definition, which credits the 1961 paper with the modern 33.7% figure.

## Factual errors
- **medium**, steps.ts:261 (dfn of "Shockley–Queisser limit"). It says the limit is "about 33.7%, found by Shockley and Queisser in 1961". The 1961 paper modelled the Sun as a 6000 K black body and found 30%: the sun and cell being assumed to be blackbodies with temperatures of 6000°K and 300°K, respectively. The maximum efficiency is found to be 30% for an energy gap of 1.1 ev. The 33.7% at 1.34 eV is the modern recalculation for AM1.5G (Rühle 2016, which model.ts already cites). Some secondary sources repeat this same mix-up. Fix: `"The highest efficiency a single-junction solar cell can reach in standard sunlight: about 33.7% at 1.34 eV. Shockley and Queisser worked out the method in 1961 (they treated the Sun as a 6000 K black body and found about 30%)."` Source: https://scispace.com/papers/detailed-balance-limit-of-efficiency-of-p-n-junction-solar-2vnks7t4if
- **medium**, steps.ts:275 (records body), "Silicon has reached 28.1% (2026), 84% of its limit". This is correct against the radiative limit. But silicon's gap is indirect, so Auger recombination sets a lower intrinsic ceiling, and a reader is left thinking 33% is within reach for silicon: The efficiency of silicon solar cells has been regarded as theoretically limited to 29.4%. Fix: append to the records notes: `"For silicon itself the ceiling is lower, about 29.4%: in an indirect-gap crystal most pairs recombine by handing their energy to a third carrier (Auger recombination) rather than by giving off light. The record is 95% of that."` Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55681-1
- **low**, steps.ts:264 (limit notes), "because the voltage rises with brightness while the glow does not." At the operating point the glow does grow with the light; at V_oc it equals the absorption. What stays fixed is the dark glow J₀. Fix: `"because the current grows in proportion to the light while the voltage also grows, by 60 mV per tenfold, so the output grows faster than the light. (This model simply scales AM1.5G; real concentrators use the direct-beam spectrum.)"`
- **low**, steps.ts:277 (records notes), "LONGi, back-contact heterojunction". LONGi describes the cell as its independently developed Hybrid Interdigitated-Back-Contact (HIBC) solar cell. Fix: `"LONGi, hybrid interdigitated back contact (HIBC)"`. Source: https://solarquarter.com/2026/04/28/28-13-26-4-longi-sets-new-world-records-for-crystalline-silicon-solar-cell-and-module-efficiency/
- **low**, records.ts / steps.ts:275–277. The records are correctly sourced to the August 2026 NLR chart, but newer certified results already exist. LONGi announced a perovskite–silicon tandem at 35.5%, certified by the European Solar Test Installation (ESTI), and LONGi has raised the HIBC cell efficiency world record from 27.81% in April 2025 to 28.29% in September 2026. This is not an error as written. Fix: re-run `build-records.mjs` once NLR adds these; keep "charted by NLR" wording so that the dated values stay accurate. Sources: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/markets-news/Newswire.ca/3310020/35-5-longi-once-again-breaks-world-record-for-crystalline-silicon-perovskite-tandem-solar-cell-efficiency/ , https://taiyangnews.info/technology/longi-raises-single-junction-silicon-cell-world-record-to-2829
- **low**, steps.ts:120, "on a panel tilted to face it". The AM1.5 surface is tilted 37°, while the Sun is at a zenith angle of 48.19°. The panel faces toward the Sun but is not perpendicular to it. Fix: `"…with the sun 48° from overhead, on a panel tilted 37° toward it."` Source: https://docs.r4photobiology.info/photobiologySun/reference/sun_reference.mspct.html
- The other claims check out: photon energies, the 1107 nm absorption edge, the AM1.5G totals (1000.4 W/m², 4.3 × 10²¹ photons, 1.45 eV average), the 60 mV per decade, the e^x(1+x) condition for the maximum power point, the pveducation figure of −2.2 mV/°C, the renaming of NREL, and De Vos's 68%.

## Model issues
- The model is sound. The J₀ prefactor 2πq³/(h³c²) is correct for E in eV with front-surface emission only. The Newton iteration and its derivative e^x(2+x) are correct. The fixed gap and the AM1.5G concentration simplification are both stated. The tests check real properties: the slices sum to 1, the limiting cases and the Rühle peak.
- **low**, e2e/solar-cells-model.e2e.ts:80. The text claims "within one percentage point from 1.1 to 1.4 eV", but the test starts at 1.12. Fix: `for (const g of [1.1, 1.12, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4])`. My estimate at 1.1 eV is about 33.2%, so the test should pass.

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **high**, steps.ts:206: "The rectangle at that point fills 87% of the largest one you could draw under the curve (V<sub>oc</sub> × J<sub>sc</sub>)". The V_oc × J_sc box, drawn dashed in IvScene, encloses the curve; the maximum-power rectangle is the largest one that fits under it. Fix: `"The rectangle at that point fills 87% of the dashed box V<sub>oc</sub> × J<sub>sc</sub>, which encloses the whole curve: the <dfn …>fill factor</dfn>."`
- **low**, steps.ts:122 (sunlight notes): "The chart plots power per 0.01 eV of photon energy". The scene divides each bin by E_STEP, and its y axis reads "W/m² per eV". Fix: `"The chart plots power per eV of photon energy (in 0.01 eV bins), so…"`
- **low**, steps.ts:175: "From now on, the cell is in full sunlight… at 27 °C". The next step changes the brightness and the one after changes the temperature. Fix: `"In this chapter the cell is an ideal silicon cell, in full sunlight at 27 °C unless you change it."`

## Catalogue requirements
- All met. The interaction is built as written: colour and intensity on a p–n junction, and a band gap that splits sunlight into four slices. The takeaway is visible on the efficiency-vs-gap curve. The real AM1.5G spectrum is used, and the pitfall is avoided: the voltage slice is computed, and the 49% ceiling is shown dashed and labelled as not the limit.

## Clarity
- **low**, steps.ts:178. The formula uses J₀ and kT/q, but J₀ is only explained in the next step's notes. Fix: add after the formula `"<p>J<sub>0</sub> is the cell's tiny dark current, the subject of the next step; kT/q is 26 mV at room temperature.</p>"`
- **low**, steps.ts:147 (heat body). The scene puts all of the excess energy on the electron, but in reality the electron and hole share it. Fix, add to the notes: `"The excess is in fact shared between the electron and the hole; the picture gives it all to the electron."`
