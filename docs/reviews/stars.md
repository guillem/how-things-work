# Review: stars

## Verdict
The page is ready after the fixes below. The model is solid: it uses real MIST tracks, real Gaia stars, Heger et al.'s burning stages and Johnson's table of where the elements come from. Almost every number the narrative quotes matches the model, and I checked many of them by hand: 6,400 L☉ at 10 M☉, 24 Myr and 4 Myr lifetimes, 170 R☉ and 2,400 L☉ at the red-giant tip, 0.54 M☉ white dwarf, 7 M☉ lost, 4.3 Mt/s, the stage durations. The problems are a handful of wrong physics statements and some internal contradictions. **The most important fix is the fusion step's claim that at 16 MK "a few [protons] collide fast enough to touch", with tunnelling only "helping".** Classically, essentially none can; tunnelling is the whole mechanism. Next is "not one [star below 0.9 M☉] has ever left the main sequence", which is true only for the Sun's composition.

## Factual errors
- **High** – steps.ts:90. "At 16 million kelvin a few collide fast enough to touch, and the strong nuclear force takes over (with help from quantum tunnelling)." Protons in the core have energies of about a keV, while the Coulomb barrier at contact is about an MeV. Practically no proton gets over it classically, so tunnelling is not a helper, it is the only route. Replace with: "Even at 16 million kelvin almost no proton is fast enough to reach another. But the fastest get close enough that, now and then, one slips through the remaining repulsion by <a href="../quantum-mechanics/#barrier">quantum tunnelling</a>, and the strong nuclear force takes over." Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08613 (the barrier "far exceeds the kinetic energy of the thermal motion" in the solar core) and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.11620.
- **High** – steps.ts:119. "Stars below about 0.9 M☉ live longer than the universe has existed: not one of them has ever left the main sequence." The 0.9 M☉ threshold is right for the model's solar composition. But old, metal-poor stars burn faster: in globular clusters, stars of about 0.8 M☉ are leaving the main sequence today. Replace with: "Stars of the Sun's composition below about 0.9 M☉ live longer than the universe has existed: none of them has yet left the main sequence. (Old stars poor in heavy elements burn a little faster; in the oldest clusters, stars of about 0.8 M☉ are leaving it now.)" Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/globular-cluster (stars "now evolving off the main sequence … is about 0.8 M⊙").
- **Medium** – steps.ts:157. "These are the carbon and oxygen of your body: made like this, in stars." The process is right, but the carbon and oxygen made in this core stay locked in the white dwarf (as the white-dwarf step itself says). The carbon that reaches space comes from helium-burning shells dredged up in giant stars, and the oxygen from massive stars (the origins step says so). Replace with: "The same reactions — in the helium-burning shells of giant stars and in massive stars — made the carbon and oxygen of your body. What this core makes stays locked inside the white dwarf."
- **Medium** – steps.ts:214. Run-step notes say massive tracks end "after carbon burning, a few thousand years before the core collapses." By the page's own Heger table, neon, oxygen and silicon burning take about 0.6 + 1.3 + 0.03 years. The 20 M☉ track ends with central carbon already down to 4% (tracks.ts:2872). So the remaining time is years to perhaps decades, not thousands of years. Replace with: "massive ones near the end of core carbon burning, only years before the core collapses." Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0211062 (Table 1).
- **Medium** – steps.ts:214. "Tracks: MIST v1.2 … stars of the Sun's composition, spinning at the start." In MIST, rotation is ramped up from zero only between 1.2 and 1.8 M☉, so the 0.5 and 1 M☉ tracks do not rotate. Replace with "spinning at the start (from about 1.5 M☉ up; MIST does not rotate stars below 1.2 M☉)". The same applies to the massive notes at steps.ts:187: say "the MIST models of 1.8 M☉ and up". Source: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/0004-637X/823/2/102 (§3.5).
- **Medium** – steps.ts:198, both the body ("for weeks as bright as a galaxy") and the supernova dfn ("can outshine a whole galaxy"). A typical core-collapse supernova (type II-P) peaks around magnitude −16.75, which is roughly a few hundred million Suns. That is about a hundredth of a large galaxy; only type Ia and rare superluminous events approach a whole galaxy. The comparison with the Milky Way at about −20.5 is from my own knowledge and not sourced; medium confidence. Replace with "for weeks as bright as hundreds of millions of Suns, rivalling a small galaxy". Source: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0004-6256/147/5/118 ("The IIP distribution was the dimmest at −16.75").
- **Low** – steps.ts:229 and origins.ts:21–22. The note says grey squares are elements "with no long-lived isotopes (technetium, promethium and those beyond plutonium)". That implies neptunium has long-lived isotopes. But Np-237 (2.14 Myr) is shorter-lived than Tc-97 (4.2 Myr), which is greyed. Either grey Np too, or reword: "technetium, promethium, neptunium and those beyond plutonium; Johnson shades polonium to plutonium by where their parent nuclei were made". Sources: https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/physics/neptunium-np/ and https://www.lanl.gov/science-engineering/periodic-table/element/technetium.
- **Low** – steps.ts:227. A Type Ia white dwarf does not "grow past 1.4 M☉": it ignites as it approaches the limit, and many Type Ia may come from mergers or below-limit detonations. Replace with "a white dwarf in a pair that gains mass from its companion, or merges with it, until it ignites and blows up".
- **Low** – steps.ts:228. "only beryllium, boron and part of the lithium were not made by stars, but by cosmic rays" leaves out the Big Bang's share of lithium (23% in origins.ts). Replace with "…and part of the lithium (with the Big Bang's share) were not made by stars: they come from cosmic rays…".
- **Low** – steps.ts:118. "The lightest stars are red dwarfs at 2,900 K." The model grid starts at 0.1 M☉, but the hydrogen-burning limit is about 0.08 M☉, and stars there are cooler. Replace with "A 0.1 M☉ red dwarf is at 2,900 K".
- **Low** – steps.ts:212. "it will still be on the main sequence when the universe is seven times its present age." The 0.5 M☉ track ends at its terminal-age main sequence (EEP 454, xH ≈ 1e-4) at 95.9 Gyr, which is 6.95× the age of the universe. Replace with "it stays on the main sequence for about 96 billion years, seven times the present age of the universe." Also fix the tracks.ts:17–18 header, which calls this the "age limit … still on the main sequence".
- **Low** – steps.ts:185. The supergiant dfn says "hundreds to more than a thousand times its size", which leaves out blue supergiants (tens of R☉). Replace with "tens to more than a thousand times its size".
- **Low** – steps.ts:93. The listed chain is only the main branch (ppI); some He-3 instead joins He-4. Add "(the main branch)".

## Model issues
- **Medium** – nearby.ts, e.g. lines 52, 540, 753, 1217, 1610, 1972. About 50–60 "ordinary" stars sit at log T 3.45–3.76 with log L between −3.9 and −6.2. That is 1.5–3.5 dex below the main sequence, where no dwarf can be, and they show up as red dots in the empty gap above the white dwarfs. They are probably cool white dwarfs that missed the WDprob cut, or faint sources with spurious BP–RP colours. Either way, the dwarf colour–temperature relation does not apply to them. In build-nearby.mjs, drop any star more than 1 dex below the zero-age main sequence at its temperature, or cut on the BP/RP excess factor, and document the cut. Add a test that no NEARBY star lies more than 1 dex below `zams`. My confidence that they are artefacts is high; on the cause, moderate.
- **Low** – star.ts:215. The black-hole remnant mass is set to `t.heCore[n]` (about 11 M☉ for 30 M☉), and LifeScene shows it as "a black hole of about 11 M☉". This assumption (the helium core falls back) is not stated in the code or on the page. Either state it in the run notes ("taken as the mass of the helium core; fallback models give a wide range") or drop the number.
- **Low** – StarScene.svelte:196, 248–249, 265. The "(model)" card hardcodes "16 million K", "160 g/cm³" and "3.8 × 10²⁶ W". The first two happen to match the model (log Tc 7.203, log ρc 2.204 at 4.57 Gyr), but the luminosity is the real Sun's: the model gives 1.11 L☉, about 4.2 × 10²⁶ W. Derive all three from `sun`, e.g. `${(sun.L * L_SUN / 1e26).toFixed(1)} × 10²⁶ W`, or relabel that line "real Sun".
- **Low** – e2e/stars-model.e2e.ts. Three problems:
  - Line 33's test name says "2% in … radius", but line 37 allows 3%.
  - The lifetime-vs-M/L loop (line 81) leaves out 60 M☉, where τL/M ≈ 48 Gyr would break its 45 Gyr bound. The note's "factor of four" is still right, but the test hides the extreme.
  - No test covers the hardcoded scene values or the HR outliers.
- The physics checks out: the equations, constants (IAU nominal values, 0.712% mass defect, AU in R☉), the burning thresholds and the Mamajek/Gaia luminosity formula are correct.

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **Medium** – LifeScene.svelte:328. Stray leftover text `0.1 ? 'var(--st-ne)' : 'var(--st-co)'} >` sits inside `<Readout id="k-co">`. It won't render in SVG, but it is a text node in the readout and may be read out by screen readers. Delete the line.
- **Medium** – steps.ts:184–185 against the CollapseScene list (lines 198–241). The massive step says hydrogen lasts "9 million years" and helium "under a million years" (from MIST). The next step's list shows "8.1 million years" and "1.2 million years" (from Heger) for the same 20 M☉ star, with no explanation. Add to the collapse step body or the list footer: "(a different, non-rotating model: its first two stages differ a little from the track you just watched)".
- **Low** – CollapseScene.svelte:113–123. The "shells still burning" loop draws the helium, carbon, oxygen/neon and silicon shells but leaves out the hydrogen-burning shell at the base of the envelope (r = 0.74). The body says "Each fuel keeps burning in a shell". Use `LAYERS.slice(0, 5)` with `r={LAYERS[k + 1].r * R}` and `appear(k + 1)`.
- All other quoted numbers match what the model and scenes compute.

## Clarity
- **Low** – steps.ts:120. "Massive stars mix more of their hydrogen into their convective cores … (a third, against a seventh for the Sun)." The Sun has no convective core, and the mass behind "a third" is not given (the test uses 20 M☉). Replace with: "Massive stars have large churning (convective) cores that mix in fresh hydrogen, so they burn a bigger share of it: a third for 20 M☉, against a seventh for the Sun."
- **Low** – steps.ts:132. "two-thirds of today's luminosity" means the model's today (1.1 L☉). Against the real Sun the young Sun was at about 75%, the familiar "faint young Sun" figure. Add "(of the model's today)" or quote 75% of the real Sun.
- The level is otherwise right: plasma, degeneracy, ZAMS, the s- and r-processes and M☉ are all defined before or where they are used.
