# Review: special-relativity

## Verdict
The page is ready once the fixes below are made. The model is correct: I re-derived the Lorentz transformation, γ, velocity addition, the light-clock tick, length contraction, the lightning events and the interval. Every number quoted in the narrative matches the model and the scene readouts. The most important fix is the GPS note in the γ step (steps.ts:113). It says that uncorrected relativity would cause "kilometres of position error within a day". The catalogue's own `gps` entry lists that claim as a pitfall: the drift is a range error, and a receiver that solves for its own clock absorbs most of it. Two other claims need correcting: that special relativity "assumes no acceleration", and the muon argument, which stops at 4.6 km and so never actually explains how muons reach the ground.

## Factual errors
- **High** — steps.ts:113 (notes of `gamma`). "Left uncorrected, the total error would build up to kilometres of position error within a day" repeats the popular claim that the catalogue's `gps` Pitfall rules out. The 7 µs/day figure from speed, and gravity's larger opposite effect, are correct. Ashby himself writes of "navigational errors of more than 11 km", but that figure is the range (light-travel) equivalent of the clock drift. The drift is shared by all satellites, so a receiver solving for its own clock bias absorbs it. GPS World puts it this way: "The position estimate would be unaffected." Replace the last sentence with: *"Gravity has the opposite and larger effect (about 45 µs a day), from general relativity, so uncorrected the satellite clocks would gain about 38 µs a day, worth about 11 km of light-travel distance. Because every satellite clock would drift together, a receiver that solves for its own clock error would turn most of this into a wrong time rather than a wrong position (N. Ashby, "Relativity in the Global Positioning System", Living Reviews in Relativity 6, 2003)."* Sources: https://link.springer.com/article/10.12942/lrr-2003-1, https://home.agh.edu.pl/~kozlow/fizyka/GPS%20i%20teoria%20wzglednosci/Relativity%20and%20the%20global%20positioning%20system_Phys%20Today%202002.pdf, https://editions.mydigitalpublication.com/publication/?i=803799&p=14&view=issueViewer
- **Medium** — steps.ts:232 (notes of `takeaway`). "Special relativity assumes no gravity and no acceleration" is a known misconception. Special relativity handles accelerating objects, and even accelerating frames; only gravity needs general relativity. The Physics FAQ says: "It's a common misconception that special relativity cannot handle accelerating objects or accelerating reference frames." Replace with: *"This page keeps to inertial frames: the train coasts at a steady speed along one straight line. Special relativity can also handle accelerating objects; what it leaves out is gravity, which needs Einstein's general relativity (1915), in which spacetime itself curves."* Source: https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/physics/Relativity/SR/acceleration.html
- **Medium** — steps.ts:125–126 (`muons`). The argument doesn't close. Muons are said to need to come from "high in the atmosphere", but the dilated range the page offers at 0.99 c (4.6 km) is still far too short. PDG puts muon production at around 15 km: "Most muons are produced high in the atmosphere (typically 15 km)". It also gives a mean energy at the ground of about 4 GeV, which is γ ≈ 38 and a mean decay length of about 25 km. After "…the muon travels about 4.6 km on average." add: *"Real cosmic-ray muons are faster still. Most are made about 15 km up, and a typical one reaching the ground has γ ≈ 40 (about 0.9997 c), which stretches its average range to about 25 km."* Source: https://pdg.lbl.gov/2022/reviews/rpp2022-rev-cosmic-rays.pdf
- **Low** — steps.ts:81. "slowed down about 100 million times" is off by about 25%. On the stage, 64 units = 1.5 m and light moves at C_PX = 160 units/s, which is 3.75 m/s. One tick (T0 = 0.8 s) is shown for a real 10 ns, so the slowdown is 8 × 10⁷. Fix: "about 80 million times".
- **Low** — steps.ts:64. "No experiment inside the train can tell whether the train is moving. Galileo saw this in 1632." Galileo's ship-cabin argument covers mechanical experiments only; extending it to every experiment, light included, is Einstein's first postulate. Fix: *"No experiment with moving objects inside the train can tell whether the train is moving: Galileo made this point in 1632, with a ship's cabin."* Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo%27s_ship

Everything else checks out: c and the 1983 definition of the metre, the 1905 postulates, 0.846 c, 10.007 ns, 1.25 and 5/3, 7.09, the jet and GPS γ values, the PDG muon lifetime 2.1969811 µs, the CERN 1977 result (γ = 29.33), 160 m, 400 ns, 3.34 ns per metre, Terrell 1959 and Einstein's train-and-embankment example.

## Model issues
- **Low** — ClockScene.svelte:80–89. Positions and counts are computed as `v * t` and `floor(t / (g*T0))` with the *current* β over the whole elapsed time. Moving the slider mid-step therefore makes the train jump, and the "count so far" readout gets rewritten after the fact; the moving clock's count can even go down. Fix: record `t0 = t` and `x0 = carU` when `target` changes, then use `x0 + v*(t - t0)`, and accumulate ticks per interval (or reset both counters when β changes).
- **Low** — SpacetimeScene.svelte:81–84. `fromTrain` duplicates `toPlatform` from the model. Similarly, ClockScene.svelte:135–137 (flash and ball hit times) and GammaScene.svelte:196–199 (the hard-coded "γ = 1.00000000000035" and "1.00000000008") keep physics outside the model and its tests. Fix: use `toPlatform({ ct: ctp, x: xp }, beta)`. Compute the two labels from `gamma(250 / C)` and `gamma(3900 / C)`; double precision is enough for β² of about 10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻¹³. Move the wall-hit times into `relativity.ts` with a test (front hit at s = hw/(c − v), back at hw/(c + v)).
- **Low** — e2e/special-relativity-model.e2e.ts:90. The "reciprocity" check `measuredLength(-0.6)` is trivially equal to `measuredLength(0.6)`, because γ is even in β. It doesn't test the platform as seen from the train. Fix: take the platform's two ends (x = ±100) at the same train time t′ = 0 via `toPlatform`/`toTrain` and assert their x′ separation is 160.
