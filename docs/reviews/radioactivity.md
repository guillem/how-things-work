# Review: radioactivity

## Verdict
The page can ship once the fixes below are made. The decay model is correct: lifetimes are exponential, survivor counts are binomial, and the dating formula is right. The nuclear data and history check out: 5,700 ± 30 y, 432.6 y, 6.0072 h, 140.511 keV, Libby's 5,568 y, IntCal20 and the 1960 Nobel Prize. The catalogue entry is fully built and the pitfall is avoided. The most important fix is the shielding demo. It draws the radiation from the specific example isotopes but labels it with generic stopping rules, so it is wrong for those isotopes. Carbon-14's betas are mostly stopped by a sheet of paper, and technetium-99m's 140 keV gammas are stopped by millimetres of lead, not "several cm". The gamma-ray definition also wrongly calls gamma rays "far more energetic" than X-rays.

## Factual errors
- **medium**, steps.ts:184 (definition of `gamma ray`). "Like light and X-rays it is electromagnetic radiation, only far more energetic" is wrong for X-rays. Gamma rays and X-rays are told apart by where they come from, not by their energy. Tc-99m's own 140 keV gamma lies in the energy range of diagnostic X-rays. Fix: `data-def="A high-energy photon emitted by a nucleus. Like light and X-rays it is electromagnetic radiation; it is called gamma because it comes from the nucleus, and it carries far more energy than light."` Source: https://www.arpansa.gov.au/understanding-radiation/what-is-radiation/ionising-radiation/gamma-radiation (gamma rays are similar to X-rays, "distinguished only by the fact that they are emitted from an excited nucleus").
- **medium**, NucleusScene.svelte:238–271 together with steps.ts:173 and the beta mode, whose example is carbon-14. The scene shows carbon-14's betas passing paper and stopping at "a few millimetres of aluminium". Carbon-14 betas have a maximum range of 0.28 mm in water, and only about 1% get through the 0.07 mm dead layer of skin. One sheet of paper (about 8 mg/cm²) stops most of them. I am fairly confident of this; the figures are standard safety-sheet values. Fix: say in the scene that this demo shows typical, more energetic betas. Change `stoppedBy` for beta to `'typical betas pass paper; a few mm of aluminium stops them (carbon-14\'s weak ones barely get through paper)'`, or add a muted label "typical beta energies, not carbon-14's". Sources: https://miamioh.edu/finance-business/physical-facilities/safety/lab-safety/radiation-safety/manual/isotope-data.html, https://www.wpi.edu/offices/environmental-health-safety/radiation/regulations/procedures/c14
- **medium**, NucleusScene.svelte:241 and steps.ts:186, gamma mode with Tc-99m. The lead barrier is labelled "lead, several cm", and the body says "it takes thick lead or concrete to absorb most of them". For the 140 keV gamma of Tc-99m, the first half-value layer is about 0.17–0.2 mm of lead, so 2 mm absorbs more than 99.9%. Several centimetres is the scale for MeV gammas such as cobalt-60. Fix steps.ts:186: `Gamma rays are the most penetrating: they pass through paper and aluminium. Lead absorbs them, a few millimetres for technetium's 140 keV rays, several centimetres for the more energetic gammas of cobalt-60, but never every single one.` Fix the barrier label: `'lead (mm to cm, by energy)'`. Sources: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2009/017881s010lbl.pdf ("first half value layer is 0.017 cm of lead"), https://www.bracco.com/media/1642
- **medium**, DatingScene.svelte:222–223 and the life view. The level is drawn as "Carbon-14 in the wood" of a living tree, held flat at 100% until the tree dies. In a tree, each growth ring stops exchanging carbon soon after it forms. Only the newest wood keeps up with the air, while the inner rings of a living tree are already decaying. This is why tree rings can be used for calibration. Fix: change the heading to `Carbon-14 in the tree's newest growth` (or in the leaves). Add to the dating notes: `In wood, each annual ring stops taking in carbon once it has formed, so a ring dates the year it grew, not the year the tree died.` Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/radiocarbon-dating
- **low**, steps.ts:187. "Long enough to deliver from a lab to a hospital" is misleading. Tc-99m is usually made on site from a molybdenum-99 generator (Mo-99 half-life about 66 h). It is the generator that gets shipped. Fix: `Short enough to fade quickly from the body; hospitals make it on the spot from a "generator" of molybdenum-99 (half-life 66 hours), which decays into it.` Sources: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK215157/, http://large.stanford.edu/courses/2026/ph241/mazumder2/
- **low**, steps.ts:146. "A speck of carbon holds billions of billions of atoms" mixes up carbon atoms with radioactive nuclei: only about 1 carbon atom in 10¹² is carbon-14. Fix: `A speck of a radioactive material holds billions of billions of nuclei.`
- **low**, steps.ts:87 (definition of `nucleus`). "About 100,000 times smaller" is the top of the range. Depending on the element, the ratio is about 10,000 to 100,000. Fix: `about 10,000 to 100,000 times smaller than the atom itself`. Source: https://spark.iop.org/episode-522-size-nucleus
- **low**, steps.ts:185. "Becomes ordinary technetium-99" can be read as stable, but Tc-99 is itself radioactive (2.1 × 10⁵ y, β⁻), as the model comment at model.ts:73 already says. Fix: `becomes technetium-99 (itself radioactive, but so long-lived that it barely adds to the dose)`.

## Model issues
- The decay law, the binomial spread, the exponential sampling, the survivor counting and `radiocarbonAge` are all correct, with correct constants and units.
- **low**, NucleusScene.svelte:110–113. The intro loop shows nitrogen-14 turning back into carbon-14 every 8 s, which is the decay running in reverse. Fix: fade the nitrogen-14 out and a fresh carbon-14 in, for example by reducing opacity around ip ≈ 7.4/8 instead of swapping the drawing in place.
- **low**, e2e/radioactivity-model.e2e.ts. No test checks the lifetime distribution itself. Add `expect(mean(makeSample(200_000, 9).life)).toBeCloseTo(1 / Math.LN2, 2)` (mean lifetime = T½ / ln 2).

Fix: `min: 0.1, step: 0.1` on `fraction`, with the clamp at line 116 changed to `clamp(..., 0.1, 100)`.
- **low**, steps.ts:117 together with GridScene.svelte:165–167. With a half-life above 20 s, the ⅛ card and its mark disappear, but the text still promises "then about 125". Fix: cap the slider at `max: 20`, or add "(if it fits in the 60 s shown)".
- **low**, DatingScene.svelte:159–168. The arrow labelled "cosmic ray" hits the nitrogen atom directly, while the body (steps.ts:215) correctly says cosmic rays produce neutrons that hit nitrogen. Fix: change the label to `cosmic-ray neutron`.

- **medium**, NucleusScene.svelte:418. "each square: one nucleus" clashes with the grid scene, where each square really is one nucleus. Here a square is a kind of nucleus. Fix: `each square: one kind of nucleus (protons, neutrons)`.
- **medium**, steps.ts:158–159. Z and A are used ("Z goes from 95 to 93, A from 241 to 237") but never defined; the isotope definition mentions the mass number but not the letter A. Fix: append to the first paragraph of the alpha step: `(Physicists write Z for the number of protons and A for the mass number, protons plus neutrons.)`
- **low**, steps.ts:230 and DatingScene.svelte:348. log₂ is not standard at level 1. Fix: `age = 5,700 years × log<sub>2</sub>(1 / fraction left), where log<sub>2</sub> counts how many halvings turn 1 into that fraction (log<sub>2</sub>(1/0.25) = 2).`
