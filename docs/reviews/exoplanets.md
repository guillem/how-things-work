# Review: exoplanets

## Verdict
The page is ready once the fixes below are made. The model is correct: Kepler III, the exact two-circle overlap (Mandel & Agol, uniform source), the K formula, the transit duration and probability, the Kopparapu 2014 coefficients, and IAU/CODATA constants. I recomputed every number the narrative quotes (84 ppm, 1 %, 13 h, 0.27°, 1 in 215, 12.5 m/s, 8.9 cm/s, about 130 m/s, Proxima's zone at 0.041–0.081 AU and 8.5–24 days, the census shares) and they hold. The census data adds up: the year columns sum to the method counts and to 6,445. **The most important fix:** on the "Only the part towards us counts" step, the velocity curve and the drawn line shift are rescaled to K. So when the reader tilts the orbit, the curve does **not** flatten as the text promises, until about 89°. A second scene bug draws the planet on the wrong half of the whole-orbit ellipse.

## Factual errors
- **medium**, steps.ts:168. "The first planets found round Sun-like stars were hot Jupiters" is not right in the plural. Of the first planets that followed 51 Peg b, 47 UMa b (Butler & Marcy 1996) has a year of about 1,090 days. Fix: "The first planet found round a Sun-like star, 51 Pegasi b, was a <strong>hot Jupiter</strong>: a giant at 0.05 AU with a year of 4.2 days, and many early finds were like it, with years of 3 to 5 days." Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.5129
- **medium**, steps.ts:267. "Venus seems to have lost its water only recently" misstates the recent-Venus limit. The limit rests on the inference that Venus "has not had liquid water on its surface for at least the past 1 billion years", and early Mars on water 3.8 billion years ago. Fix: "…the more optimistic limits, from the evidence that Venus has been dry for at least a billion years, but perhaps not much longer, when the young Sun was fainter, and that Mars had rivers 3.8 billion years ago." Source: https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0004-637X/765/2/131/meta
- **medium**, GlareScene.svelte:5–7, 177–191. Two labels treat the intercepted fraction (R/2a)² as "an upper bound on what they reflect" toward us, and as "all the light it could send us". That only holds for total reflected power. The flux an observer sees is A_g (R/a)² Φ(α). At full phase that is 4·A_g times the intercepted fraction: about 1.5× for the Earth (A_g = 0.367) and about 2× for Jupiter (A_g = 0.52). Fix: in the m-ratio readout, replace "bigger than all the light it could send us" with "all the light it catches from the Sun". In the docstring, say "(R / 2a)², the light they intercept". The glare note "less than a billionth" is still true (about 6.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ at most). Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.5099, https://radiojove.gsfc.nasa.gov/education/educationalcd/Posters&Fliers/FactSheets/JupiterFactSheet.pdf
- **low**, steps.ts:141. "The few planets that have been photographed directly are huge, young…" is no longer universal. JWST imaged Epsilon Indi Ab, a cold (about 275 K) planet round a star 3.7–5.7 billion years old. Fix: "Most of the few planets that have been photographed…". Source: https://www.sci.news/astronomy/webb-super-jupiter-epsilon-indi-ab-13125.html
- **low**, steps.ts:279. "The first exoplanets of all" ignores earlier unconfirmed claims such as γ Cep Ab in 1988. Fix: "The first confirmed exoplanets, found in 1992, orbit a pulsar…". Source: https://www.inverse.com/science/what-was-the-first-exoplanet
- **low**, steps.ts:253. "The planets' radii are known only from their transits, and their masses only from the wobble" does not fit 51 Peg b. Its archive radius is "calculated by the archive" from a mass–radius relation, and its true mass needs the tilt measured from the planet's own spectrum, as the reveal says. Fix: "Where a planet transits, its radius comes from the dip; its mass comes from the wobble (for the one that does not transit, with a tilt measured later from the planet's own light)." I'm only moderately confident about the reveal's "the mass above is the true one" (ReadScene:524). Published tilts for 51 Peg b have disagreed (roughly 70–80° in earlier papers, against the 49.8° here), so "an estimate of the true one" would be safer.

## Model issues
- The equations, constants and units are all correct and the tests check real-world properties. One addition would help: a test that a planet larger than its star gives depth 1.
- **low**, ReadScene.svelte:135, 214–245. On the mystery step, the dip handle is live for the planet that never transits (51 Peg b, up to 0.1 %). Dragging it produces a size for a planet with no dip. Fix: `const sizeRead = $derived(hits && readDepth > 0 ? radiusFromDepth(readDepth, st.radius) : NaN);` and render the depth line and Handle only `{#if mysteryStep && hits}`.
- **low**, TransitScene.svelte:94–95. If the planet is bigger than the star (22 R⊕ round Proxima gives k ≈ 1.4), `yLo` becomes −30 % and the axis shows negative brightness. Fix: `const yLo = $derived(Math.max(0, 100 * (1 - 1.3 * scaleDepth)));`

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **high**, WobbleScene.svelte:90–97, 114. The text says "Tilt the orbit … the curve flattens" (steps.ts:223). But `vMax = max(K, 0.02·vTrue)·1.35` makes the y-axis follow K, so the curve keeps its height until sin i < 0.02 (tilt > 88.9°). `mag` is also recomputed from K, so the line shift stays 25–60 px. Only the axis numbers change. Fix: on the tilt phase, scale both to the star's true speed:
  `const ref = $derived(phase === 'tilt' ? vTrue : Math.max(K, 1e-9));`
  `const vMax = $derived(Math.max(ref, 1e-9) * 1.35);` and use `ref` in place of `K` inside `mag` and in the `yt` ticks.
- **medium**, TransitScene.svelte:155–163. The whole-orbit ellipse halves are flipped relative to the planet. `ellipseHalf(true)` draws the near half at `OCY + ry·cos θ` (below the star). But the planet dot (`oPos.y = OCY − ry·cos θ`) and the close-up track (`pos.y`) put the near side above the star. As a result, the planet in front rides the dashed "far side" arc and goes behind the star along the solid "near side" arc. Fix: `(OCY - ry * Math.cos(th))` in `ellipseHalf`, and update its comment to "drawn above the star".
- **medium**, WobbleScene.svelte:366–374. Face-on (tilt slider 100, so K = 0 and v = 0), `Math.abs(v) < K*0.05` is false, and the label reads "away from us: redder by 0 nm" in red. That contradicts "the lines don't shift at all". The title also reads "drawn 100 000 000 000× larger". Fix: `Math.abs(v) <= Math.max(K * 0.05, 1e-12)` for the label and colour. When K < 1e-6, show "no shift: seen face-on" in the spectrum subtitle instead of the magnification.
- **low**, WobbleScene.svelte:318. The strip starts at L0 = 385 nm, but its left edge is labelled "400 nm". Fix: `<T x={sxL(400)} … text="400 nm" anchor="middle" />`, or label the edge "385 nm".

## Catalogue requirements
- Everything is met. Size, mass, orbit and tilt drive both curves. The lines shift. Size and mass are read back, from the model and by hand on real archive planets. The habitable zone uses Kopparapu 2014. The census makes the takeaway visible ("Seen: fewer than 2 in 100"), and the data are real and documented. The only caveat is the tilt-flattening fix above.

## Clarity
- **low**, steps.ts:210. "The shift is the speed divided by the speed of light" confuses the shift with the fractional shift. Fix: "The shift, as a fraction of the line's wavelength, is the speed divided by the speed of light…".
- **low**, steps.ts:210. "the Doppler effect you met with sound" assumes waves-interference, which is only Related, not a prerequisite. Fix: "the <a …>Doppler effect</a>, the same one that makes a passing siren drop in pitch."
- **low**, steps.ts:196. "it takes the Sun just outside its own surface" is ambiguous. Fix: "so the balance point lies just outside the Sun's surface, and the Sun circles it at 12.5 metres per second…".
- **low**, steps.ts:224 (minimum-mass definition). "mass × sin(inclination)" uses trigonometry, which this Level-1 page doesn't assume. Fix: "…the mass a planet would need if we saw its orbit edge-on; the more tilted the orbit, the more the true mass exceeds it."
- **low**, CensusScene.svelte:162. "All the others were found from … their star's light" doesn't cover pulsar timing (radio pulses) or disk kinematics. Fix: "Almost all the others…".
- **low**, ZoneScene.svelte:155. "to scale" doesn't apply to the star dot, which is drawn about 20× too big for the Sun. Fix: "orbits to scale, star enlarged".
