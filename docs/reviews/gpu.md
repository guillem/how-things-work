# Review: gpu

## Verdict
The page is ready once the fixes below are made. I ran the model again outside the page, and every number it computes matches what the text promises: 140,900 ticks for the fast core; 623 ticks with 1,024 lanes (226×); 50 ticks with 16,384 lanes (2,818×, lanes busy 69% of their ticks against 88%); about 7.1× with 32 lanes; warp costs of 11, 21 and 25 ticks; a shader intensity of about 4.08 operations per byte; ridges at 82 and 27.4 operations per byte; Amdahl gives 19.6× and 20.0×. The published figures for the RTX 4090 and the i9-14900K are correct. The most important fix is to say, on the matrix step, that the race treats memory as free. Without that, the step calls the naive matrix product "the perfect job" (exactly 4,096× faster), and two steps later the roofline shows the same naive product stuck waiting for memory. A close second: the claim that the CPU does 16 32-bit multiply-adds per core per tick "on average" is wrong.

## Factual errors
- **medium**, steps.ts:225 (also gpu.ts:358–362 comment): "Intel's published figure works out to 16 32-bit multiply-adds per core per tick, **on average**." This is not an average. Intel's formula gives every one of the 24 cores the big cores' rate (16) and the big cores' 3.2 GHz base clock. The 16 small Gracemont cores split each 256-bit AVX2 operation into two 128-bit halves, so they do about 8 per tick, and their base clock is 2.4 GHz. The real mean is (8·16 + 16·8)/24 ≈ 10.7. Fix: "Intel's published figure counts 16 32-bit multiply-adds per tick for every core. That is true of the 8 big cores; the small ones do about half." In gpu.ts, replace "an average, not a per-core spec" with "the big cores' rate applied to all 24; the small cores do about half". I am fairly confident about the Gracemont width; confirm it against Intel's optimization manual if you can. Sources: https://chipsandcheese.com/p/gracemont-revenge-of-the-atom-cores, https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/products/sku/236773/intel-core-i9-processor-14900k-36m-cache-up-to-6-00-ghz/specifications.html
- **medium**, RooflineScene.svelte:367 footnote: "(all 24 cores at 3.2 GHz; they can run up to 6 GHz)". Intel lists an efficient-core base of 2.4 GHz and an efficient-core maximum turbo of 4.4 GHz. Only the performance cores reach 6 GHz (P-core max turbo 5.6 GHz, Turbo Boost Max 3.0 5.8 GHz, Thermal Velocity Boost 6 GHz). Fix: "* twice Intel's published 64-bit figure, 1,228.8 GFLOP/s (Intel's formula counts all 24 cores at the big cores' 3.2 GHz base; the big cores boost to 6 GHz, the small ones to 4.4 GHz): an AVX2 register holds 8 floats or 4 doubles". Source: the Intel specification page above.
- **low**, steps.ts:255: "165.2 trillion operations a second on 16-bit numbers … twice its ordinary peak". Whitepaper Table 2 gives 165.2 TFLOPS for FP16 tensor work *with FP32 accumulate*. With FP16 accumulate it is 330.3 (four times the ordinary peak), and FP8 reaches 660.6. Fix: "…165.2 trillion operations a second on 16-bit numbers summed in 32-bit (82.6 trillion multiply-adds, twice its ordinary peak; 330.3 trillion if the sums are kept in 16-bit too), without NVIDIA's 'sparsity' doubling." Source: https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/Data-Center/l4/nvidia-ada-gpu-architecture-whitepaper-v2.1.pdf
- **low**, steps.ts:227: "about 1.3 trillion a second, no faster than the CPU". The model's own figures are 1.29 against 1.23 trillion, so the GPU is slightly faster. The test (line 51) checks a ratio of 1.05. Fix: "about 1.3 trillion a second, about the same as the CPU."
- **low**, specs.ts:8 and steps.ts:227 cite the whitepaper as "V2.02", but the URL is v2.1. Fix: cite "V2.1" in both places, or link the v2.02 document.

## Model issues
- **medium**, gpu.ts:21–22 / steps.ts:211: the rule "memory is free: no task ever waits for data" appears only in a code comment. The catalogue requires simplifications to be stated on the page. Here it matters: each matrix element costs 3n + 2 instructions, with both loads counted as one tick, and that is what produces "exactly 4,096 times faster". The tiles step (line 252) then says the same naive product "lands on the sloping line", waiting for memory. Fix: add to the matmul notes: "In this race every load takes one tick, as if memory were free. Done this naive way, a real GPU would spend most of its time waiting for those loads; the next chapter shows why, and how tiles fix it." Also add "and memory costs nothing" to the race notes at line 157.
- **low**, RaceScene.svelte:93 and :178: the slow panel draws its finished pixels as the first `groupsDone × 32` pixels. Groups actually finish out of order (a sky group costs 11 ticks, a ball group 21, an edge group 25), so mid-race the picture fills a prefix that does not match the model. The counts and percentages are right. Fix: build the clip from the groups whose `end[g] ≤ ticks`, and highlight the groups with `start[g] ≤ ticks < end[g]` as in progress.
- **low**, e2e/gpu-model.e2e.ts: two numbers written by hand in the text are not tested. One is "7 times faster" with 32 lanes (steps.ts:156; the model gives 7.13). The other is the fast core winning "past about a quarter" (steps.ts:282). Fix: add `expect(Math.round(runFast(px).cycles / runLanes(px, 32).cycles)).toBe(7)`, plus checks that `contest(0.24, 1024).winner === 'lanes'` and `contest(0.26, 1024).winner === 'fast'`. Better still, compute both numbers in steps.ts.

## Narrative and scene mismatches
- **medium**, steps.ts:225 and RooflineScene.svelte:67 ("Cores (each fetches its own instructions)": 24 against 128 SMs). The page has already taught that "A GPU shares one control unit between 32 lanes" (line 182), and the chips card says each SM has 4 warp schedulers that issue separately. By the page's own definition, the 4090 has 512 units that fetch their own instructions, not 128. Fix in the text: "The fair count is 24 CPU cores against 128 SMs (or 512 warp schedulers, if you count every unit that issues its own instructions)". Fix in the table label: "Cores (CPU) / streaming multiprocessors (GPU)".
- **low**, steps.ts:155: "The fast core has barely started its first rows when the lanes finish". With 1,024 lanes it has shaded 1/226 of the image, about 181 pixels, which is less than one 256-pixel row. Fix: "The fast core hasn't finished its first row when the lanes are done".
- **low**, steps.ts:268 against :281: "16,384 only 20.0 times" (the real value, 19.98, rounded) sits next to "no number of workers ever goes beyond 20 times". Fix: use `n(…, 2)` for the 16,384 figure, which gives "19.98", or write "just under 20 times".
- **low**, steps.ts:282: "past about a quarter … the fast core wins" holds for the default of 1,024 workers. With 4 workers or fewer, the fast core wins at any serial share. Fix: "With a thousand or more lanes, push the serial share past about a quarter…".

## Catalogue requirements
All met. Both jobs are raced (pixels, then matrices), the serial-share slider makes the speed-up hit its ceiling, the takeaway appears on its own on the final card and in the races, and the prerequisites cpu and linear-transformations are linked in the text.

## Clarity
- **low**, steps.ts:182: SIMT is spelled out as "single instruction, multiple *threads*", but the page never says what a thread is; it only talks about lanes. Fix: add "(each lane runs one thread, one copy of the program on its own data)".
- **low**, steps.ts:240: "Our pixel shader does about 4 per byte and still waits for memory" can puzzle a reader, because the shader reads nothing. Fix: "Our pixel shader does about 16 operations for the 4 bytes of colour it writes, about 4 per byte, and still waits for memory."
